Two questions, two answers. As you'd run it (each engine's default threading), it's a crossover — Ibex owns small data and sliding windows; Polars' multithreading takes large aligned. Per core (everyone pinned to one thread), Ibex wins every single config — so its kernels are the most efficient of the three, and every loss above is thread count, not algorithms.

Same query, POLARS_MAX_THREADS=1 and DuckDB threads=1. This is the fair "how good is the engine" comparison — and Ibex leads at every scale and both window types.

The headroom. On this workload Polars and DuckDB each convert one thread into ~3.3–3.9× throughput. Ibex converts it into 1×. Give Ibex even a 3× parallel speedup and 5M-aligned goes 575→~190 ms (past Polars' 284) and 5M-sliding 583→~195 ms (past Polars' 556). The single-threaded lead is the evidence that multithreading turns the remaining losses into wins.
